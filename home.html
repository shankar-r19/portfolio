Recent Work

Tango

Tango is a cooperative online quiz game that pairs a Republican and a Democrat as teammates. Partners chat in real time to agree on answers, and the questions are designed so that each side's knowledge helps the team win. Across five randomized controlled trials, playing Tango for under an hour reduced political animosity, with effects lasting up to four months.

Woodley, DeFilippis, Ravi & Greene, “Defusing political animosity in the United States with a cooperative online quiz game,” Nature Human Behaviour (2025).

GAZE

GAZE is an iOS app that turns focus into a game. Each distraction-free session keeps a streak alive and grows a visual journey, such as a metro map or a constellation, so progress is something you can see. The app notices when you reach for your phone mid-session and nudges you back. Rooted in cognitive science and mindfulness research, it has no feeds or manipulative notifications, and all progress stays on your device.

Brain Rat

Your apps, locked behind a very small rat. Brain Rat pairs a phone app with a palm-sized collectible figure that hides an NFC chip in its base. Pick the apps you want to lock, put your rat somewhere inconvenient, and tap your phone on its head to unlock them for 15, 30 or 60 minutes. The walk is the point: by the time you've crossed the room, the urge has usually passed. It works without accounts or the cloud, using Screen Time on iPhone and a block screen on Android.

Series 01 has nine rats, sold blind-boxed. Each one is a thing you could be doing instead.

Not a Rat, the companion browser extension, is out now. It interrupts a doomscroll by locking the scroll buttons for five seconds while you take a breath.
